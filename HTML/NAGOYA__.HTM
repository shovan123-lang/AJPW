ALL JAPAN PRO WRESTLING: SUMMER ACTION SERIES REPORT
Nagoya, Aichi Prefectural Gymnasium | July 11, 1991

CHAMPIONS HOLD THE LINE IN NAGOYA

Both sets of champions walked out of the Aichi Prefectural Gymnasium
still champions on Thursday night, but neither title match came easy,
and the undercard left at least one young American with a story he will
be telling for years.

In the main event, World Tag Team Champions Mitsuharu Misawa and
Toshiaki Kawada turned back the most dangerous team they have faced all
tour. Rick Rude and Abdullah the Butcher gave the champions twenty-three
minutes of chaos, with Abdullah going to the eyes more than once and
Rude working Kawada's leg at every opportunity. It was Misawa who found
the opening, catching Rude with the Running Elbowsmash to retain the
titles. Abdullah was still arguing with the referee as the champions'
music played.

The All Asia Tag Team Championship stayed with Can-Am Express, but
Yoshinari Ogawa and Masao Inoue made them work for every minute of it.
Ogawa in particular has been the story of this tour, and he looked every
bit like tomorrow's champion before Dan Kroffat finally silenced him
with a moonsault bodyblock. Doug Furnas and Dan Kroffat retain, but the
challengers left Nagoya with the crowd fully behind them.

JUMBO'S ARMY SENDS A MESSAGE

Triple Crown Champion Jumbo Tsuruta did not wrestle alone tonight. With
Akira Taue and Kenta Kobashi at his side, Jumbo faced down the Miracle
Violence Connection of Steve Williams and Terry Gordy, with young
Richard Slinger rounding out the opposing side. It did not go well for
Slinger. Jumbo finished him with the Jumbo Knee in just over ten
minutes, a clear reminder to Williams and to everyone else in the
building of exactly who still runs this tour.

TWO FALLS, NO ANSWER FOR ACE

Dean Malenko needed the full allotment to settle things with Johnny Ace,
taking the first fall with a side suplex before Ace was counted out
chasing the win in the second. The Malenkos have now beaten every junior
AJPW has thrown at them this July, and there is no name left on the
tour that looks like a real test.

AROUND THE RING

Too Cold Scorpio opened the show with a Flying Scorpio Splash to put
away Rusher Kimura. And in a tour that has mostly kept its two toughest
gaijin apart from trouble, Stan Hansen and Dan Spivey fought the British
Bulldogs to a double countout, with Dynamite Kid and Hansen trading
shots well after the bell had stopped meaning anything.

The tour rolls on to Hiroshima on July 13.
