Kumamoto Prefectural Gymnasium
Kumamoto
July 17, 1991
Attendance: 2,500
Average ticket: $20.00
Gate: $50,000
Merchandise: $5,000

KOBASHI MOONSAULTS RUDE, AND MISAWA HAS HIS NEW PARTNER

Two nights after Toshiaki Kawada walked out of All Japan, Mitsuharu Misawa
came to the ring in Kumamoto to tell the fans who would stand beside him
now. He didn't need to say much. Kenta Kobashi had just beaten Rick Rude
with the moonsault in the best match of the night, and the answer was
already in the ring.

KOBASHI'S NIGHT

Rude came to Japan as a star from America and has spent the tour looking
for a big win. He nearly had one tonight. He hit the Rude Awakening late
and Kobashi got a shoulder up at two and a half, and the crowd started
chanting Kobashi's name. A few moments later Kobashi climbed to the top and
came down with the moonsault for the pin at 15:11. It is his biggest
singles win of the summer, two nights after Jumbo Tsuruta needed the
backdrop driver to keep the Triple Crown from him.

Then Misawa walked out. He took the microphone, said Kawada's name once,
then raised Kobashi's hand. Super Generation goes on. Misawa and Kobashi
challenge the Miracle Violence Connection for the World Tag titles in
Yokohama on July 23.

MIRACLE VIOLENCE ROLLS ON

The champions warned them what's coming. Steve Williams and Terry Gordy,
with Dory Funk Jr. as their partner, beat Jumbo Tsuruta, Akira Taue and
Masanobu Fuchi in a fierce six-man that went 18:47. Williams finished Fuchi
with the Doctor Bomb. Jumbo was left in the ring holding his neck while
Williams pointed at him from the aisle. Their Triple Crown match at Budokan
is ten days away.

CAN-AM KEEP THE GOLD

The Can-Am Express made their fifth All Asia Tag defense, against the
British Bulldogs. Dan Kroffat finished Davey Boy Smith with the tiger
driver at 15:06. The Fantastics are next, at Budokan.

ACE TAKES THE RUBBER MATCH

Johnny Ace and Too Cold Scorpio settled their series at last. Ace caught
Scorpio with the Implant DDT at 14:03 and leads the series two wins to one.

THE MALENKOS' WAY

Dean and Joe Malenko beat Yoshinari Ogawa and Tsuyoshi Kikuchi when Dean
rolled Kikuchi up and put his feet on the ropes at 15:31. The referee never
saw it. The young juniors were furious.

AROUND THE RING

Haruka Eigen returned from his elbow injury, and Abdullah the Butcher
welcomed him back with a second-rope elbow drop for the pin at 9:56.
Abdullah and Giant Kimala II beat Eigen and Rusher Kimura. In the opener,
Billy Joe Travis got revenge on Masao Inoue for their Hiroshima match with
a reverse small package at 10:06.
